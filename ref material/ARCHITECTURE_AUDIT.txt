================================================================================
1. SUMMARY
================================================================================

The architecture is sound. The layering the design argues for — corpus-derived
policy, a single escalation gate, calibration at bucket level, drafting as the
only fallible node — is implemented as described, and the invariants that matter
are enforced structurally rather than by convention.

Four real defects were found. None is a design flaw; all four are localised and
each is a few lines to fix. Two of them (3.1, 3.2) would produce a WRONG NUMBER
IN THE WRITEUP rather than a crash, which makes them the most serious kind of
bug this project can have — the failure mode BUILD.md's "eval/results/ is
committed" rule exists to prevent.

The dominant risk to the submission is not in this list. It is that the
classifier is undertrained (macro-F1 0.6035, one class at F1 0.000) while every
component around it is sound. See section 7.

2.1 src/triage/schemas.py — 476 lines
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The strongest file in the repository. Its distinguishing property is that the
routing invariants are enforced in pydantic validators rather than in the
router's control flow, so a future edit to route.py cannot violate them without
raising.

Verified enforced:
  - _always_escalate_buckets_escalate: REQUIRES_ACCOUNT_LOOKUP, HIGH_CONSEQUENCE
    and NO_SUPPORTING_SOP cannot construct a non-escalate decision at ANY
    confidence, including 1.0.
  - _reason_matches_action: an escalated item must carry a reason; a non-escalated
    one must not.
  - _redirect_only_for_out_of_scope: REDIRECT is unavailable to any other bucket,
    and AUTO_REPLY unavailable outside auto_answerable.
  - _check_distribution: probabilities must sum to 1.0 within 1e-3, and the
    predicted label must exist in the distribution.

The Email/ground-truth separation is correct and worth keeping. Email declares
extra="forbid", so a corpus row (which carries label, source_sops, plant_pii and
eight other generation fields) cannot be loaded as an Email. This is deliberate:
a real inbound citizen email has no `label` field, so if Email accepted extras a
node could read email.label, score perfectly in evaluation, and be impossible to
deploy. Forbidding extras converts that mistake into an immediate failure at the
seam instead of an inflated metric. eval/dataset.py holds ground truth in a
SIBLING object (Labelled.email / Labelled.label), so the runtime path cannot
reach it even accidentally.

redacted() is a genuine persistence boundary: it substitutes the scrubbed text,
empties the vault, drops the sender, and — importantly — falls back to blanking
the body entirely when scrubbing never ran, rather than passing the original
through on the assumption it was clean.

2.2 src/triage/graph.py — 232 lines
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    scrub -> classify -> calibrate -> retrieve -> route -+-> draft    -> END
                                                         +-> declined -> END

Six nodes, one conditional edge, dependencies injected via GraphDeps so nodes
stay pure(state) -> partial state and tests construct graphs with stubs and no
weights on disk.

The conditional edge is the escalation gate made structural: an escalated item
does not reach the drafting node because NO EDGE LEADS THERE, not because of an
early return inside the drafter that a later edit could delete.

Fail-safe verified by injecting a classifier that raises:
    classifier crash -> escalate | no_supporting_sop | in_human_queue: True
    errors: ['classify failed: model died',
             'calibrate skipped: no classification',
             'retrieve skipped: no classification',
             'routed without a prediction: escalating']
    draft: not_attempted
Each node degrades independently, records why, and the item reaches a human.
Nothing guesses.

2.3 src/triage/nodes/route.py — 192 lines

Eight escalation reasons in documented precedence. Reasons 1-7 are structural and
would fire identically at probability 1.0; only reason 8 (low_confidence) reads
the model. The threshold clamp from BUILD.md S9.9 is intact — verified that a

2.4 src/triage/nodes/calibrate.py — 124 lines
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Contains the subtlest correct decision in the codebase, and it is worth stating
because it is easy to get wrong and impossible to notice when wrong:

    The predicted bucket is the bucket of the PREDICTED CLASS, not the argmax of
    the summed bucket distribution.

These differ. An email at account_specific 0.40 with three auto-answerable
classes at 0.20 each has an auto_answerable bucket mass of 0.60 — larger than
the 0.40 on the bucket that actually applies. Taking the argmax bucket would
auto-reply to an enquiry about a real taxpayer's record. The code follows the
class; test_rollup_follows_the_class_not_the_argmax_bucket pins it.

log(0) is guarded via _EPSILON for buckets holding no probability mass.

2.9 The two misfile guards (added this session)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
foreign_income_dta and scam_report were declared in SOP frontmatter but
unenforced. Both are now wired. Measured on the 520-email test split:

    foreign_income_signal   P 1.000   R 0.762   support 42
    scam_signal             P 1.000   R 0.976   support 42

Precision 1.000 on both means zero false positives, so they cost no coverage.
They are misfile guards rather than cross-cutting conditions: they matter only

documented S10 deferral, and tests/test_schemas.py now asserts that every wired
flag keys on a trigger some indexed SOP actually declares.

3. REAL DEFECTS — fix before reporting any number

3.1 summary.json asserts reportability that config/thresholds.yaml denies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEVERITY: high — this is the exact failure BUILD.md S7 exists to prevent.
LOCATION: eval/run_eval.py:330

    "calibrated_numbers_reportable": scaler.fitted,

The flag is set from scaler.fitted alone, which is True because the temperature
WAS fitted (T=0.6831, n=401). But config/thresholds.yaml line 15 still reads:

    # STATUS: PLACEHOLDER. These are not fitted values.

and the values are still the placeholder 0.5 / 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.0 / 1.0. Confirmed
in the committed summary.json:

    calibrated_numbers_reportable: True
    thresholds: {'auto_answerable': 0.5, 'out_of_scope': 0.5, ...}

So the committed results file states that its numbers are reportable while the
configuration that produced them states they are not. Whichever is believed, one
is wrong, and the flag exists specifically to stop a placeholder figure being
mistaken for a measured one.

WHY IT MATTERS: coverage and risk both depend on the threshold. Every
risk-coverage number, the AURC, and the operating points are currently computed
under an unjustified 0.5 cutoff. They are placeholder results wearing a
"reportable" label.

FIX: two parts.
  (a) Choose the operating thresholds from the risk-coverage curve on the test
      split, write them into thresholds.yaml, and remove the PLACEHOLDER banner.
  (b) Make the flag mean what it says — read the banner (or a `fitted: true` key)
      out of thresholds.yaml and AND it with scaler.fitted, so the flag cannot be
      True while the config says placeholder.

3.2 parse_citations can attribute the wrong SOP and leak a stray line
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEVERITY: high — corrupts the grounding metric AND the citizen-facing reply.
LOCATION: src/triage/nodes/draft.py:102  (regex at :62)

    match = _CITED_RE.search(text)

.search() returns the FIRST match. _CITED_RE is anchored (^\s*CITED:) under
re.MULTILINE, so mid-sentence text is safely ignored — that much is fine. But any
CITED: line appearing at a LINE START before the drafter's own is taken instead.

Reproduced:

  input:  "You wrote:\nCITED: SOP-REL-004\n\nMy answer.\nCITED: SOP-REL-001"
  cited:  ('SOP-REL-004',)          <-- WRONG, the model grounded on REL-001
  body:   still contains a 'CITED:' line

  input:  "Answer.\nCITED: SOP-REL-004\nMore.\nCITED: SOP-REL-001"
  cited:  ('SOP-REL-004',)          <-- WRONG
  body:   still contains a 'CITED:' line

Two distinct harms:
  (1) grounded_on records the wrong SOP, so grounding accuracy (BUILD.md S9.2) is
      measured against a value the drafter did not choose.
  (2) only the matched line is stripped, so the OTHER CITED: line survives into
      draft.text — internal SOP identifiers shipped to a citizen.

TRIGGERS: a citizen quoting a previous reply back at IRAS (ordinary in
correspondence), or a verbose model emitting two citation lines. Neither is
exotic.

FIX: take the LAST match and strip ALL of them.

    matches = list(_CITED_RE.finditer(text))
    if not matches: return text.strip(), ()
    cited = ... from matches[-1] ...
    body  = _CITED_RE.sub("", text).strip()

3.3 pii_recall stores raw unredacted PII into a committed file
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEVERITY: medium — currently latent, becomes a real leak on the first miss.
LOCATION: eval/metrics/pii_recall.py:111 (populate), :49 (serialise)

    missed_by_type[pii_type].append(str(value))   # the raw planted value
    ...
    "missed_examples": list(self.missed_examples)

The purpose is debugging — knowing WHICH values escaped detection. But the output
is written to eval/results/scrub_recall.json, which is committed to git by
design, so the module whose job is measuring PII containment is the one module
that writes raw PII to disk.

It is empty today ONLY because recall is currently 1.0000. The first missed value
commits an identifier. The corpus is synthetic so no real person is exposed, but
the pattern is exactly backwards and would be indefensible in a writeup about PII
handling.

FIX: store a masked form and keep the diagnostic value —
    "S0433218J" -> "S****218J", or record (type, length, first/last char).
Alternatively gate the raw list behind an explicit --debug-pii flag that never
writes to eval/results/.

3.4 encoder_vs_llm computes macro-F1 over a data-derived label set
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEVERITY: medium — makes the headline model-selection comparison unsound.
LOCATION: eval/ablations/encoder_vs_llm.py:87

    report = classification_report(truth, predicted)     # labels= omitted

With labels omitted, classification_report derives the label set from the union
of truth and predictions actually seen. On a small stratified sample the two
models see DIFFERENT label sets — a class the encoder never predicts but the LLM
does (or vice versa) changes the macro denominator — so the two macro-F1 figures
are averages over different numbers of classes and are not comparable.

Quantified:
    derived labels ('filing','payment')               -> macro_f1 0.3333
    fixed   labels ('filing','payment','tax_reliefs') -> macro_f1 0.2222
    discrepancy 0.1111 on the same predictions

This directly affects the encoder-vs-LLM table, which is the evidence for the
model-selection deliverable.

FIX: pass the fixed 12-class set to both calls —
    classification_report(truth, predicted, labels=tuple(encoder.labels))

4.1 Quadratic backtracking in the currency-question pattern
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LOCATION: src/triage/nodes/flags.py:71  (_CURRENCY_QUESTION)

NOTE — this corrects an earlier verbal report in which I attributed the slowdown
to _ACCOUNT_PATTERNS. Isolating each pattern individually shows the account
patterns are fast; _CURRENCY_QUESTION is the sole cause.

The [^.?!]* segments on either side of the first-person marker backtrack against
input with no sentence terminator. Measured on "my " * n + "refund?":

        n_words   seconds
            750     0.060
           1500     0.257
           3000     0.999
           6000     4.619

Clean quadratic — doubling the input quadruples the time. Real citizen emails are
~2KB and complete in milliseconds, so this is not a live problem; it is a
denial-of-service shape in a public-facing intake path, which is a different
thing from a bug.

FIX (cheapest): cap the text length before flag detection, e.g. detect_flags
operates on text[:4000]. A citizen email longer than that is already anomalous.
Alternatively bound the wildcards — [^.?!]{0,200} — which preserves intent, since
a first-person marker 200+ characters from the currency figure is not the
"whose facts" signal the pattern is trying to catch.

4.2 No minimum-content guard
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A one-character body ("x") flows through the whole pipeline and reaches
auto_reply if the classifier is confident. Verified with a stub forcing 0.9.

The real MiniLM would probably be unconfident on such input and the threshold
would catch it — but that is a hope about model behaviour, not a property. Every
other safety rule in this system is structural, and this one is not.

FIX: escalate below a minimum token count with a new reason (insufficient_content),
or document explicitly that the confidence threshold is the intended guard.

5. SECURITY REVIEW
================================================================================

Checked and CLEAN:

  Prompt injection via email body — SAFE. Email text is passed as an ARGUMENT to
  str.format(), never as part of the template, so braces in a body are inert.
  Verified with bodies containing "{sops}", "{}", "{0}" and "{x}" — none raised
  or interpolated. (This is worth keeping in mind if anyone later switches to
  f-strings or .format on user-supplied templates, which would break it.)

  Label injection via the LLM baseline — SAFE. _parse matches replies against the
  known label set, so a model answering "tax_filing" cannot create a thirteenth
  class. Reasoning blocks (<think>...</think>) are stripped before parsing so a
  model's deliberation, which names several categories, cannot be mistaken for
  its conclusion.

  Placeholder fabrication — SAFE and deliberate. A draft containing a placeholder
  the vault never issued (e.g. [NRIC_7]) fails the draft rather than shipping a
  bare token. Covered by test_fabricated_placeholder_fails_the_draft.

  Secret handling — CLEAN. .env untracked and gitignored; env.py returns only
  variable NAMES, never values; key_status() reports booleans. No key is logged
  or serialised.

  Committed artefacts — CLEAN. All 11 JSON files under eval/results/ scanned for
  NRIC and SG phone patterns: zero hits.

Residual risks, both stated rather than fixed:

  - PII detection is regex plus a street gazetteer. Free-text addresses without a
    street-type token are the known gap. Recall is MEASURED (1.0000 on planted
    values) rather than claimed, which is the right posture — but planted PII is
    PII the generator knew to plant, so this measures detection of known shapes,
    not of everything a citizen might write.
  - See 3.3 — the module measuring containment currently writes raw values.

7. THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST
================================================================================

Everything in sections 3 and 4 is localised and cheap. The real problem is
elsewhere and is not an architecture problem:

    macro-F1 (12 classes) ......... 0.6035
    oos_other_agency .............. F1 0.000 over 41 test examples
    payment ....................... F1 0.257
    coverage at <=10% risk ........ 2.3%

A class that is never once predicted correctly across 41 examples is a training
failure, not a weak class. Investigated and ruled out: label alignment is correct
(labels.json is sorted, matching train_encoder.py), and the splits are clean
(grouped by scenario_id, zero bleed). The signature is undertraining —
training.json shows calibration accuracy plateauing at epoch 3 (0.7357) and
FALLING at epoch 4 (0.7282) with train loss still at 0.98.

The consequence for the writeup is direct: coverage of 2.3% at a 10% risk
tolerance does not support a claim that a meaningful share of the inbox can be
automated. The mechanism is sound and well-tested; it is being fed weak
predictions.

Related, and awkward: the LLM zero-shot baseline currently BEATS the encoder.

                        encoder    llm 0-shot
    accuracy             0.667        0.833
    macro-F1             0.655        0.826
    ECE                  0.308        0.124
    sec/email            0.631        8.643
    distinct conf vals      36            2

(n=36 stratified, k=3. Subject to 3.4 — recompute with a fixed label set.)

The architectural argument survives: 2 distinct confidence values against 36 is
the resolution cap at 1/k, which no amount of data removes, and that is the real
case for an encoder when the threshold is the product. But "the encoder is better
calibrated" is currently FALSE as measured, and the writeup cannot assert it.
Either the classifier is retrained, or the model-selection section is rewritten
around the resolution/latency/cost argument and reports the accuracy gap
honestly.

One further note for the build log: the fitted temperature is 0.6831, which
SHARPENS rather than flattens. This is the opposite of BUILD.md S5.5's premise
that raw softmax is overconfident. The cause is the 12->5 rollup: probability
mass scattered across several classes within one bucket sums to an
UNDER-confident bucket score. Worth recording as a genuine finding — it is a
consequence of the bucket design that the design did not anticipate.
